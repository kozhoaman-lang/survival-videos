## SCENE 2 — WHERE ARE YOU? (0:25–1:15)

VISUAL: Map zoom-out: modern USA → continent shifts → a warm coastal plain with rivers and a shallow sea to the east. Label: `Hell Creek — Montana & the Dakotas`.
SFX: Map whoosh, soft synth pad.

> First, where are you?
> Scientists call this place Hell Creek. Today it's dry badlands in Montana and the Dakotas. But right now it's a warm, swampy lowland... full of rivers, ferns, conifers, and some of the very first flowering plants.
> Look around. Something is missing. There are no open meadows of grass. The grassy plains you know haven't spread across the world yet.
> And it's hot. Even in winter, this place barely gets cold.
> That sounds like good news. It isn't. Heat means you lose water fast. And water is where your first problem starts.

## SCENE 3 — DAY 1: WATER (1:15–2:30)

VISUAL: The hero walks to a wide brown river. Calm surface. Then two eyes and nostrils rise in the water. Crocodile silhouette. He steps back.
SFX: Water lapping, sudden silence, low cello note.

> Day one. You need water... and you can hear a river.
> You walk toward it. Brown, slow, calm.
> Too calm.
> Hell Creek's rivers were home to crocodile relatives and alligator-like hunters. They waited right where thirsty animals came to drink. Animals like you.
> So you don't drink from the edge.
> Instead, you go a few steps back from the bank, where the ground is sandy, and you dig. A shallow hole. Slowly, water seeps in from the river... filtered through the sand.
> It's still not perfectly safe. But it's cleaner, and you didn't have to put your face next to something with teeth.
> You drink. You survived day one.
> But tonight, you'll learn this world doesn't sleep.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 1: DON'T DRINK AT THE EDGE`
EDIT NOTE: Close-up of the seep hole slowly filling — satisfying shot, good for a Short.

## SCENE 4 — DAY 2: FOOD (2:30–3:45)

VISUAL: Night → dawn. The hero is hungry (stomach growl icon). He looks at red berries, at strange ferns, at a nest of large eggs. Then at turtles on a log in the river.
SFX: Stomach growl (comic beat), birds that sound wrong, a big distant call.

> Day two. You're starving.
> Your first idea: eat plants. Bad idea. You don't know a single one of them. Some ferns are toxic raw, even today. In a world you've never seen, a mistake with plants can kill you slower... but just as surely.
> Your second idea: dinosaur eggs. You find a nest. The eggs are huge.
> Stop. Ask yourself one question... where is the mother?
> Many dinosaurs guarded their nests. Stealing eggs is the fastest way to meet an adult.
> So you do what early humans did best. You go small.
> Turtles basking on logs. Fish in the shallows. Freshwater clams. Hell Creek was full of them.
> The safest meal in the age of dinosaurs... isn't a dinosaur at all.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 2: EAT SMALL`

## SCENE 5 — DAY 3: THE GENTLE GIANTS (3:45–5:00)

VISUAL: The hero hides behind ferns. A Triceratops steps into the clearing. It looks peaceful, chewing. He walks closer. Its head turns. Horns lower. He freezes, then backs away slowly.
SFX: Heavy footsteps, chewing, a short snort, tension strings.

> Day three. You hear chewing.
> Through the ferns you see it. Triceratops. One of the most common big dinosaurs in Hell Creek.
> It eats plants. It looks calm. You think: this one's safe.
> That thought gets people killed... even today.
> In Africa, the animals that kill the most people aren't lions. Hippos and buffalo are among the most dangerous animals on the continent. Plant-eaters. Big, nervous, and protective.
> Now imagine one nine meters long, weighing several tons... with three horns and a bony shield.
> It doesn't need to hunt you. It only needs to feel threatened.
> You back away slowly. You never turn your back. You never get between a herd and its young.
> And when you're far enough away... you realize something else is moving in the trees.
> Something that's been following you.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 3: PLANT-EATER ≠ SAFE`
EDIT NOTE: End scene on the rustling bush + two small eyes. Cut to black. Strong retention point.

## SCENE 6 — DAY 4: THE HUNTERS IN THE DARK (5:00–6:20)

VISUAL: Dusk. Feathered silhouettes between trees. A small raptor (Acheroraptor) steps into the light — dog-sized, feathers, sharp claw. The hero climbs a tree. Two raptors circle below, then leave.
SFX: Clicks, soft chirps, branches cracking, heartbeat.

> Day four. You're being watched.
> Forget the giant raptors from the movies. The real hunters of Hell Creek were smaller. One of them is called Acheroraptor. Feathered. Fast. About the size of a big dog... with a curved claw on each foot.
> A big dinosaur might ignore you. To something this size, you're exactly the right size.
> Your choice: run or climb.
> You climb.
> Raptors were built for running, not climbing tall trees. You wedge yourself high between two branches and you stay there... all night.
> Below you: clicks. Chirps. Footsteps that stop... and start again.
> At sunrise, they're gone.
> You come down shaking. You think the worst is over.
> But something already got you. You just can't feel it yet.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 4: GO UP`

## SCENE 7 — DAY 5: THE INVISIBLE ENEMY (6:20–7:30)

VISUAL: Close-up of the hero's arm: a tiny tick. Mosquitoes. Zoom into a cartoon "microscope" circle full of strange microbes. He sweats, shivers, sees blurred shapes.
SFX: Mosquito whine, muffled heartbeat, distorted ambience.

> Day five. You wake up with a fever.
> Remember the footprint? Remember the raptors? None of them got you. Something tiny did.
> Ticks were already feeding on feathered dinosaurs tens of millions of years before this day. Scientists have found them trapped in amber, holding on to a dinosaur feather. Mosquitoes were here too.
> And then there are the microbes. Bacteria and parasites that evolved in a world with no humans. Your body has never met any of them.
> Nobody knows exactly what they would do to you. And that's the scary part.
> You spend the day in the shade, drinking from your seep hole, keeping your cuts clean with boiled... no. You have no pot. You have no fire.
> You just wait... and hope.
> By night, the fever breaks.
> And for the first time since you woke up, the jungle goes completely quiet.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `THE SMALLEST THINGS ARE THE MOST DANGEROUS`

## SCENE 8 — DAY 6: THE KING (7:30–9:00)

VISUAL: Silence. Birds fly away. Ground shakes in rhythm. A huge shadow covers the hero. Tyrannosaurus rex head slowly comes through the trees, sniffing. Split screen: "MOVIE MYTH" vs "SCIENCE".
SFX: All ambience cuts out. Deep footsteps. Huge breath. Then music explodes when he runs.

> Day six. Silence means one thing in this world.
> Something big is coming.
> Tyrannosaurus rex. Twelve meters long. One of the strongest bites of any land animal that ever lived.
> And you remember the movies: stand still... it can't see you if you don't move.
> That's a myth.
> Studies of its skull suggest T. rex had forward-facing eyes with good depth perception... and possibly sharp eyesight. It also had huge smell centers in its brain. It doesn't need you to move. It can smell you.
> So standing still is the worst thing you can do.
> Here's the one thing on your side: size. A full-grown T. rex was so heavy that some scientists estimate its top speed at only around twelve miles per hour. A fit human can sprint faster than that... for a short time.
> But young tyrannosaurs were lighter, leaner... and probably much faster.
> So you don't run in the open. You run where it can't follow. Through thick trees. Across the river. Anything that breaks its line of sight and its path.
> You dive into the water... and you stay under as long as you can.
> When you come up... it's gone.
> You did it. Six days. One more and you've survived the age of dinosaurs.
> Tomorrow should be easy.

## SCENE 9 — DAY 7: THE TWIST (9:00–10:45)

VISUAL: Peaceful morning, warm light, Triceratops in the distance, birds. Then: a strange flash on the southern horizon. Minutes later, the ground trembles. Tiny glowing glass beads fall like hail. The river surges backward over its banks. The sky turns orange.
SFX: Calm music → sudden silence → low rumble → rain of tiny "tink" sounds → roaring water. Music: slow, sad, big.

> Day seven. Spring morning. The air is soft. The animals are calm.
> And then... far to the south, the sky does something wrong.
> Remember how this started? Sixty-six million years ago.
> That's not just a date. That's THE date.
> Three thousand kilometers away, on what will one day be Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, a rock about ten kilometers wide has just hit the Earth.
> You can't see the impact from here. But within minutes, the ground starts to shake.
> Then something falls from the sky. Not rain. Tiny beads of glass... melted rock, thrown into space and raining back down.
> This isn't imagination. In North Dakota, at a fossil site called Tanis, scientists found fish with those glass beads stuck in their gills. They were breathing them in... as they died.
> The river beside you rises and rushes backward over its banks.
> Some scientists believe the sky itself may have grown hot as debris fell back through the atmosphere.
> The dinosaurs around you have ruled this planet for more than one hundred and fifty million years.
> They have only hours left.
