VISUAL: Glowing streaks fall across the sky. The air shimmers. Animals run in panic. The hero sinks into the river up to his nose, then pushes into a muddy bank under roots.
SFX: Crackling sky, distant roar, muffled underwater sound.

> Above you, the sky is glowing.
> Billions of tiny pieces of rock, blasted into space by the impact, are falling back through the atmosphere.
> Some scientists think this rain of debris may have heated the air for a short time... possibly enough to start fires in some places. Others think the heat was much weaker. Nobody knows for sure.
> But you're not going to bet your life on it.
> So you do what the survivors of this day did. You get low. Under water. Under mud. Under the ground.
> You push yourself into the riverbank, under the roots of a tree, and you wait.
> It's not a plan you came up with. It's the plan that crocodiles, turtles and small burrowing animals were already living by.
> And it's about to save your life.

## SCENE 4 — DAY 10: THE SUN GOES OUT (2:50–4:05)

VISUAL: The sun becomes a dim orange disk, then barely visible. Midday looks like evening. Leaves on plants start to droop and brown. Split screen: "BEFORE" green forest / "AFTER" gray.
SFX: Low drone, music in a minor key.

> Day ten. It's noon... and it looks like evening.
> The impact threw huge amounts of dust, soot and sulfur high into the sky. And it's spreading around the whole planet.
> Some studies suggest that sunlight was dimmed so much that plants around the world struggled to grow — maybe for many months, maybe longer.
> And here's why that's so deadly.
> No sunlight, no plants. No plants... no food for plant-eaters. No plant-eaters... no food for the hunters.
> The whole food chain is collapsing. Not with an explosion. Slowly. Quietly.
> And you're part of that food chain.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `NO SUN → NO PLANTS → NO HERBIVORES → NO PREDATORS`

## SCENE 5 — DAY 14: THE COLD (4:05–5:20)

VISUAL: The hero shivers. His breath fogs. Frost on ferns — in a place that never had winter. He builds a dugout shelter in the riverbank, lines it with dry leaves.
SFX: Wind howling, teeth chattering, crunching frost.

> Day fourteen. You wake up shaking.
> Remember how hot this place was? Even winter here was warm.
> Now... your breath turns to fog.
> Scientists call it an impact winter. With the sky blocked, the planet's surface cools down fast. Climate models suggest it could have stayed much colder than normal for years.
> For animals built for a warm world, this is a death sentence.
> But you know something they don't. You know what cold does to a body. So you get out of the wind, you get off the bare ground, and you dig in.
> A hole in the riverbank. Dry leaves underneath you. Dry leaves on top of you.
> You're living like a burrowing animal now.
> And that's the second secret of who survives.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 2: DIG IN`

## SCENE 6 — DAY 18: WHAT DO YOU EAT NOW? (5:20–6:35)

VISUAL: Dead leaves and rotting plants in the river. Insects, snails, worms in the muck. Fish and turtles still alive in the water. The hero fishes with a sharpened stick.
SFX: Water drips, buzzing, a splash.

> Day eighteen. You're starving again.
> On land, almost nothing is growing. But look at the river.
> Something strange is happening there. Rivers and lakes are full of dead plants, rotting leaves, sinking debris. And that dead matter feeds worms, insects, snails... which feed fish and turtles.
> Scientists think this is one reason why freshwater animals did better than many animals on land. Their food didn't depend only on fresh green plants.
> So you stay by the water. You eat what the river gives you.
> It's cold. It's dark. It's disgusting.
> But you're still alive.
> The giants around you... are not.

ON-SCREEN TEXT: `RULE 3: STAY BY FRESH WATER`
